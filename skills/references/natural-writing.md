# Natural Prose Check (lightweight checklist)

A self-check that Web-Publish, Game-Publish, Web-DeepSearch, and Game-DeepSearch run on their own output right before delivering it. Don't rewrite from scratch or call another tool — just look at what you just wrote once more and fix only the sentences that trip these patterns.

**This only applies to prose.** Tables, callouts, and toggles are structure, not prose — they're there on purpose for visibility, so leave them alone. What this catches is whether the sentences inside that structure read like they were AI-written.

## What to scan for

- **Mechanical parallel repetition**: the same connector pattern repeated throughout a paragraph (Korean example: "~하고, ~하며, ~합니다" chained identically sentence after sentence; in English, watch for the same "X, which Y, and Z" shape repeated). If the same pattern shows up 2-3+ times, vary at least one sentence's structure.
- **Overused transition words**: a transition word glued onto nearly every sentence (Korean: "또한", "그러나", "따라서", "이처럼"; English: "furthermore", "however", "therefore", "in this way"). Drop them where the flow holds without them.
- **Cliché filler**: generic, content-free lines like "이는 매우 중요한 부분입니다" / "this is a critically important consideration." Replace with a specific reason or number, or just cut it.
- **Monotonous rhythm**: every sentence at the same length and shape reads mechanically. Mix short and long sentences.
- **Unnecessary bilingual glosses**: don't bracket an English term next to a Korean word that's already commonly used on its own (an exception applies when the English term really is the standard jargon).
- **Over-hedging**: double-softened phrasing ("~일 수도 있을 것으로 보입니다" / "it may possibly be the case that..."). State it plainly when there's a basis for it; if it's genuinely uncertain, flag it once, clearly, as an estimate.

## Judgment call

Don't audit every sentence — only touch it when one of the patterns above shows up **noticeably repeated**. Once or twice is normal even in natural writing. The goal isn't a perfect "human voice," it's making sure a skim doesn't feel like it came off an assembly line.

Note: the plan/report content itself is normally written in whatever language the user is using (Korean, by default, for this skill set's usual audience) — this checklist applies in that output language, not in English. The Korean examples above are the primary case; the English equivalents are given so the pattern is recognizable if a user works in English instead.
